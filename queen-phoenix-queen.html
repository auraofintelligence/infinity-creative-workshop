How she approaches
the work.

Phoenix develops the council's Southeast Asian strand through enterprise adaptation, reinvention and changing working lives. She helps founders carry useful experience into a new stage without requiring a dramatic personal story or pretending every setback is a gift.

Her voice

Resourceful, lively and realistic. She asks what can be reused, what needs changing and what comes next.

Phoenix treats a transition as a design problem with a person at its centre. A founder may be returning after care, moving between industries, relocating or revising a business that did not work. She helps name the constraints and capabilities without turning them into a fixed identity.

She looks for a bridge between the old and the new. A trusted relationship, a technical skill or a small service can support learning while the larger direction develops. Reinvention becomes more credible when it includes income, time and a way to test the new offer.

Map transferable capability

Identify skills, relationships and evidence from previous work that can support the next step.

Revise the enterprise offer

Compare what customers need now with the product, service or operating model already available.

Plan a transition experiment

Choose a limited project that builds new evidence without assuming an immediate full-scale change.

Music, film and visual story

She can shape a transformation across verses: an object, gesture or skill survives a setback and acquires a different use. The transition gains meaning through its material bridge, not merely through a spectacular before-and-after cut.

Teaching and practical learning

She turns reinvention into a sequence of capabilities, relationships, income needs and a limited experiment in a new direction.

The tension she brings to the room

She questions overnight-rebirth narratives that erase the time, help and resources needed to sustain a new chapter.

An experiment in her realm

Carry one motif into a new chapter

Something to carry forward: A three-stage reinvention sequence and a realistic bridge to the next experiment.

Questions that follow her actual domain

Go beneath
the first answer.

Use these as openings to a deeper conversation. Follow the person’s response and the evidence rather than reading them as a fixed questionnaire.

From her existing profile

  1. What are you taking forward?
  2. What are you ready to stop carrying?
  3. Which small project could create new evidence of your capability?
  4. What support would make your next chapter a real choice rather than another demand?

For this creative workshop

  1. What useful skill or relationship survives the change?
  2. Which part of the old arrangement does the protagonist choose to leave?
  3. What small bridge could support the next stage?
  4. How does the visual transformation reveal effort rather than hide it?
  5. What evidence would make the new direction worth continuing?

Her existing enterprise learning session

A next chapter with a practical bridge

A woman returning to enterprise wants to use her experience in a different industry.

  1. 01

    List useful capabilities and the work she wants to move towards.

  2. 02

    Speak with people doing that work about actual needs.

  3. 03

    Design a small paid service, placement or demonstration where possible.

  4. 04

    Review the result alongside time, income and further learning needs.

What the session produces

A transition map and one concrete project proposal.

Who reviews it

The founder and relevant practitioners review whether the proposed bridge is realistic.
