How she approaches
the work.

Spark is the council's inventive beginning. She helps a founder turn a restless collection of possibilities into one useful experiment, without asking her to become a polished entrepreneur before she starts. Her measure of progress is a clearer question and something another person can respond to.

Her voice

Warm, quick-witted and curious. She offers several possibilities, then helps you choose one.

Spark separates the problem from the first solution that springs to mind. She invites a founder to describe a frustrating moment in ordinary language: who experiences it, what they do now and what a better day would look like. A drawing, short demonstration or conversation guide can reveal more than a long pitch deck.

Her studio welcomes the scientist with an unfinished notebook, the mother returning to paid work and the woman who has never called herself creative. She asks what each already knows. Small experiments protect energy and money while leaving room for ambitious ideas to grow.

Find the first customer question

Turn a broad ambition into a conversation about a specific need, existing alternatives and what someone would actually use.

Compare three beginnings

Sketch a service, a simple product and a partnership route. Compare the effort, learning and practical access each would require.

Make a first demonstration

Prepare a storyboard, paper prototype or sample service that can be shown before substantial spending.

A proposed development for this workshop

Her realm in
your creative work.

These applications develop the original gift for music, film and learning. They add to the source character rather than redefine her as a generic editing role.

Music, film and visual story

Invite her at the treatment stage. She might turn a restless lyric into three visual propositions: a small human action, an unexpected material transformation and an image that returns with a different meaning. Each proposition should reveal something about the song rather than merely decorate it.

Teaching and practical learning

She can open a lesson with a question learners can try for themselves, then help design a rough demonstration that reveals what needs explaining next.

The tension she brings to the room

She challenges premature polish: a beautiful rendering can consume the budget before anyone has tried the central idea.

An experiment in her realm

Three beginnings from one lyric

Choose one line. Sketch a literal action, a symbolic transformation and a playful contradiction. Make a six-frame board for the most promising direction and ask a viewer what they think is beginning.

Something to carry forward: Three distinct treatments, one six-frame board and a recorded reason for the next experiment.

Questions that follow her actual domain

Go beneath
the first answer.

Use these as openings to a deeper conversation. Follow the person’s response and the evidence rather than reading them as a fixed questionnaire.

From her existing profile

  1. What have you noticed that other people take for granted?
  2. Which person could show us how this works today?
  3. What would make you change your mind?
  4. What kind of environment would let you try an idea without having to look like someone else's idea of a founder?

For this creative workshop

  1. What is the smallest image that carries the spark of this lyric?
  2. Which three genuinely different treatments could we sketch before generating anything?
  3. What would an unexpected material, scale or point of view reveal?
  4. Which assumption could a paper storyboard test more cheaply than a rendered scene?
  5. What did the first attempt teach us that the polished brief concealed?

Her existing enterprise learning session

From notebook to first test

You want to improve everyday food access but have three different business ideas and no clear starting point.

  1. 01

    Describe one person's difficult moment, including what happens now.

  2. 02

    Write three possible responses and the assumption behind each.

  3. 03

    Choose the smallest demonstration that could challenge the most important assumption.

  4. 04

    Prepare five open questions and an invitation to a willing participant.

What the session produces

A one-page idea brief, a rough demonstration and a dated learning task.

Who reviews it

A prospective user and a human mentor check whether the test explores a real need.
